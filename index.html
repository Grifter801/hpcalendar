HP Calendar

A calendar of dates from the Wizarding World. Support and contact.

SupportPrivacy Policy

HP Calendar shows you what happened on today's date in the Wizarding World — 70 events drawn from the seven novels and the author's supplementary notes, then checked against the Harry Potter Lexicon's own entries. You can browse by day, search the lot, get a daily note, or copy every anniversary into your own calendar.

Common questions

How do I turn on the daily notification?

Settings tab → On This Day Alerts, then pick a time. iOS will ask permission the first time. Only 44 days of the year have anything recorded on them, and the rest stay silent — you will not get an empty notification. Days with several events, like 2 May 1998, arrive as one notification rather than four.

I ran the calendar export twice and now everything is duplicated.

That is a real limitation and not something you did wrong. Add to my calendar writes the anniversaries afresh every time it runs; it keeps no record of what it added before, so it cannot skip them.

The reason is the permission it asks for. It requests write-only calendar access, which lets it add events but makes it technically incapable of reading your calendar back — so it genuinely cannot see that its own events are already there. Keeping the permission narrow was the deliberate choice; not de-duplicating is the price.

To clean up, delete the duplicates in the Calendar app. If you would rather manage the import yourself, use Create calendar file instead — it hands you a standard .ics file, and you decide what happens to it.

Why do fewer events export than the app contains?

Because not every date is known to the day. 61 of the 70 events have a real day of the year and become recurring anniversaries; the other 9 are known only to a month, a year, or approximately — the founding of Hogwarts, the Statute of Secrecy. Rather than invent a day for them, the app keeps them as a browsable timeline and leaves them out of the export.

What do the “year only” and “circa” labels mean?

That the app knows the date less precisely than a day. Canon dates are unevenly known: some are stated outright, some are pinned only to a month or a year, and some are reconstructions worked back from the school calendar. Each event declares what it actually knows rather than rounding everything to a tidy date.

Dates that are reconstructions but still name a specific day carry no label — there are enough of them that badging every one put a caveat on a large share of the list for a difference of a day or two. Instead, the event's note explains the reasoning, so you can judge it yourself.

Where do the dates come from?

The seven novels first, then the author's supplementary notes. Every entry was then checked against the Harry Potter Lexicon's own pages, which found and fixed real errors — including several dates that were correct in themselves but credited to the wrong source, and one widely-repeated “fact” that turned out to trace to a single reconstruction the Lexicon itself labels as not canon.

Where a date is inferred rather than quoted, the entry says so and explains why. A few birthdays circulate online with more confidence than their sourcing warrants, and the app tries not to add to that.

iOS keeps asking me to confirm an icon change.

Changing your house in Settings changes the home screen icon to match, and iOS shows that confirmation every single time an app's icon changes. There is no supported way for an app to suppress it.

Gryffindor is the exception: it uses the app's primary icon, so selecting it involves no icon change and no alert.

I found an error in an event.

Please report it. Include the event title and what you believe the correct date or detail is, with a source if you have one. Corrections are welcome and are the main way the dataset improves.
